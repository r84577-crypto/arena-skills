# Code of conduct

Participation in this repository — issues, pull requests, discussions, reviews —
is conditional on conduct that makes technical disagreement productive. This is an
original code written for a small project, not the Contributor Covenant; if you want
a widely adopted standard instead, the [Contributor Covenant 2.1](https://www.contributor-covenant.org/version/2/1/code_of_conduct/)
is the usual choice and this text holds nothing against it.

## Expected

- **Attack claims, not people.** Evidence first. "This bypasses the checker, here is
  the file that proves it" is the shape of a useful message; "this checker is a joke"
  is not.
- **State your uncertainty.** "I assumed X; if that is wrong, ignore this" saves a
  round trip and is a mark of skill, not weakness.
- **Separate taste from defect.** Formatting preferences are fine to raise; they are
  not blockers, and they are not findings.
- **Accept "no".** A rejected contribution is a decision about the repository, not a
  verdict on you. Pushback is welcome in the thread that decided it.

## Not acceptable

- Harassment, insults, or sustained disruption; sexualised attention or imagery.
- Discriminatory comments about who someone is, or where they are from.
- Publishing another person's private information without permission.
- Submitting real credentials, personal data, or someone else's unpublished work.
- Using this project's issue tracker to promote a product or to run a campaign
  against another project or person.

## Scope

This applies in repository spaces (issues, pull requests, reviews, commit messages)
and whenever you are representing the project elsewhere.

## Enforcement

Report conduct problems privately to the maintainer through
[GitHub's private reporting](https://github.com/r84577-crypto/arena-skills/security/advisories/new)
— it is the only private channel this project has. Reports are read by the maintainer
alone and kept confidential.

Responses are proportionate: a private clarification, an edit or withdrawal of a
comment, a temporary block from the tracker, or a permanent ban. The maintainer may
remove or edit any contribution that breaks this code, and will say why.

The maintainer is also the enforcement contact, so there is no appeal past a decision.
That is a real limitation of a one-person project; it is stated here rather than
disguised as a process.
